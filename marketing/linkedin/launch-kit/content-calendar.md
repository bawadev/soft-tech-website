## Article Outlines

### Outline 1 — What "platform-grade" actually means
**Audience:** CTOs, Heads of Engineering, founders scaling past MVP
**Length:** 1,400–1,800 words

1. **The word "platform" lost its meaning.** Everything is a platform now. Most of it isn't.
2. **What separates a platform from a product.** Multi-tenant by design, not retrofit. Real-time primitives. Horizontal scale as a first-class constraint.
3. **The four loads a platform carries.** Traffic. Time. Change. Trust. Most teams engineer for the first and hope for the other three.
4. **Architecture decisions that age well.** Event-driven by default. API-first, even internally. Database choices you can live with for ten years.
5. **The unglamorous engineering.** Observability. SLOs. Disaster recovery. Security review cadence. None of it ships a feature. All of it keeps you in business.
6. **How we evaluate a platform before we touch it.** A short checklist we use in week one of every engagement.
7. **Close.** A platform isn't a milestone. It's a commitment.

### Outline 2 — The ten-year architecture
**Audience:** Engineering leaders deciding between rebuild and evolve
**Length:** 1,200–1,500 words

1. **The three-year rewrite cycle is not a law of physics.** It's a cultural choice. You can choose differently.
2. **Why systems get rewritten.** Lost context. Coupled code. Fear of the core. Each is preventable.
3. **Architecture as a contract with your future team.** What changes when you assume the maintainer is someone you'll hire in 2032.
4. **Patterns that survive.** Clear boundaries. Documented decisions. A budget for evolution every quarter.
5. **Modernization without stopping the business.** Incremental, strangler-fig migration. How we run two systems in parallel for years.
6. **What it costs to build this way — and what it costs not to.**
7. **Close.** Decades sound dramatic until you've lived through the alternative.

*Internal links: Partnership pillar, Modernization service, Contact.*

### Outline 3 — Why understanding people is the part that can't be automated
**Audience:** Product leaders, founders, CMOs
**Length:** 900–1,200 words

1. **The technical barrier fell.** Code is now the easy part.
2. **What's actually hard about building software people use.** Predicting choice. Earning trust. Designing for how people feel, not how specs assume they behave.
3. **Consumer psychology is not a buzzword.** It's a discipline. Most products don't use it.
4. **How discovery actually works.** Interviews. Observation. Decision-journey mapping. The slow part most teams skip.
5. **Why this work still belongs to people.** Not because AI can't help — it can. Because trust is built between people, and products inherit it.
6. **Close.** Build for your customers, not your requirements.
